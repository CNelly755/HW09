## Koot, "The VOC, the Dutch East India Company, 1602–1799"

URL: https://bpb-us-w2.wpmucdn.com/sites.umassd.edu/dist/4/628/files/2017/02/thevoc.pdf

What it says, in my words:

- Share prices reached record highs in the 1720s while the dividend yield was about 3.4 percent.
- By the 1730s the shares were like bonds, paying three to four percent, with little hope of capital gains.
- From the 1730s profits were about two to three percent of revenues.
- Between 1690 and 1760 the company paid out more in dividends than it earned. After 1730 it drew down capital and borrowed short-term.
- Koot reports de Vries and van der Woude's judgement that profits were "vanishingly small after 1730".
- From 1740 to 1780 the Company needed about 28 guilders of subsidy from Europe for every 100 guilders of Asian revenue.
- Its strengths were in the inter-Asian trade. Clove plantations on Banda were worked by enslaved people. It also took part in an opium syndicate.

Used in the paper: the whole "Dutch company looks safe and is not" paragraph; the Banda clove sentence; the claim that the slave economy was centred on Batavia and Banda (Banda is in Koot; **Batavia's slave population is not in my notes from Koot, so that half of the sentence needs a source or should be cut**).

Caution: the 28-guilder subsidy figure is not used in the paper because I could not tell whether it was a ratio of Europe's remittances or an accounting construct.

## Robins, "This Imperious Company", Gresham College, 27 June 2008

URL: https://www.gresham.ac.uk/watch-now/imperious-company-east-india-company-and-modern-multinational

- The Company received its charter from Elizabeth I in 1600.
- Twenty-four directors were elected by shareholders.
- The share price passed £200 around 1717, in the period of the firman and the South Sea Bubble.
- The 1720s to the early 1740s were a "golden age" with steady dividends.
- From about 1740 into the early 1750s there was a French threat and falling dividends.
- The Company shared Leadenhall Street with the Royal African Company.

Used in the paper: the structure paragraph, the "steady until about 1740" paragraph, the turn after 1740, and the Royal African Company remark in the ethics section.

Caution: this is a lecture transcript. It gives no table of prices or dividend rates, so the paper does not give any.

## NBER Working Paper 21536 (cited as Bogart in the paper)

URL: https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w21536/w21536.pdf

- The 1730 charter act: the Company paid £200,000 to George II and received a lower interest rate on the roughly £3.2 million debt owed by the government. A rival petition failed in the Commons by 223 to 138.
- In 1744 the Company lent £1,000,000 at 3 percent to George II and got a monopoly extension.
- The Company was forced to lend in every war up to 1750.
- Company directors in the Commons were 67 percent Whig 1722–27, 28 percent 1727–34, 75 percent 1734–41, and 83 percent 1741–47.

Used in the paper: the 1730 and 1744 sentences. The Whig percentages are not used.

Caution: I did not record the paper's title and author. Copy them from page 1 before submission.

## Treaty of Aix-la-Chapelle, 18 October 1748

URL: https://collections.ubcic.bc.ca/files/original/93b97089194dee1dcad63d8515db9d634a3ad4d3.pdf

- Article V: general restitution of conquests, in both the East and West Indies.
- Article IX: arrangements about Cape Breton and commissioners for the East and West Indies.
- Article XVI: confirms the Asiento for the Negro trade.
- Dutch plenipotentiaries Hasselaer and Van Borssele are themselves VOC directors.

Used in the paper: Article V (restoration of conquests), Article XVI (Asiento). The paper's phrase "returns conquests made in the East and West Indies" follows Article V; I did not write the name Madras next to the article because the article does not name it.

The last point, that two of the Dutch signatories were VOC directors, is a nice detail for a future draft. It shows the company's weight in Dutch diplomacy.

## Stellwagen, "Valckenier en Van Imhoff" (Dutch)

URL: https://dbnl.org/tekst/_els001189501_01/_els001189501_01_0020.php

- Quotes Valckenier's letters to the Heeren XVII on the Batavia killings of 9–10 October 1740: "niet minder dan 10,000 Chineezen".
- The arrest of the councillors on 6 December 1740.
- The Seventeen annulled the arrests.
- Valckenier himself was arrested at the Cape on 25 January 1742 and tried at Batavia. The criminal demand of March 1744 included a charge carrying death.
- He died in prison on 20 June 1751 (page 228).

Used in the paper: the whole Batavia paragraph.

Caution: Stellwagen wrote in 1895. He is an old secondary source who quotes primary letters. I cannot say whether he quotes them correctly. Blussé's open-access book (2025) is the proper modern check, and I only read its abstract.
